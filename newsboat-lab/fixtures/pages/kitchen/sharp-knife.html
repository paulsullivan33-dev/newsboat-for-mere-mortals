A sharper knife is a safer knife

The Sample Kitchen Journal — a fixture page invented for this guide's lab.

A dull knife slips off an onion skin and looks for a finger instead. A sharp one goes where you point it, so the cutting takes less force and the force never surprises you.

A few strokes on a steel before you start keeps the edge straight. A proper sharpening — a whetstone, or a professional with one — a few times a year restores an edge the steel can no longer save.

Store the knife where the edge touches nothing hard: a magnetic strip, a block, or a drawer with a guard. The dishwasher is the fastest way to undo all of the above.
