Watering tomatoes without drowning them

The Example Garden Notes — a fixture page invented for this guide's lab.

Tomatoes like a deep drink twice a week, not a shallow sip every day. Water at the base of the plant, early in the morning, and keep the leaves dry — wet leaves invite the blights that end tomato seasons early.

A layer of mulch — straw, shredded leaves, or grass clippings — slows the soil drying out between waterings. In a hot week, check the soil with a finger: if the top five centimetres are dry, water; if they are still damp, wait a day.

Uneven watering is what splits the fruit and rots the blossom end. Steady and deep beats frequent and shallow, every time.
